How ThetaMask stays private

A privacy tool should be easy to check. Here is exactly what it does, what it can see, and where its limits are.

The short version

  • Everything happens in your browser, on your computer.
  • ThetaMask has no servers. It makes no network requests at all: no analytics, no crash reports, no update checks of its own (your browser handles updates).
  • There is no account and nothing to sign in to.
  • EmbedTheta never receives your text, your client names or your settings.

What happens when you paste

  1. Detection, on your computer. Rules with checksums find structured details such as card numbers (Luhn check), IBANs (mod-97 check), National Insurance numbers, emails, phone numbers and API keys. A small AI model (GLiNER PII Edge, 46 MB, open licence) finds names, companies and addresses. Your own always-hide list is matched too.
  2. Swapping. Each real detail is replaced with a realistic stand-in of the same kind. Stand-ins come from ranges reserved for examples wherever they exist: 555-01xx and Ofcom drama phone numbers, published test card numbers, example IBANs, example.com emails, documentation IP addresses. So a stand-in never points at a real person, card or account.
  3. Remembering, briefly. The table linking each real detail to its stand-in is kept for that chat only, in your browser's session storage. It is wiped when you close the browser, or straight away from Settings.
  4. A check before sending. If you type something new and press Send, ThetaMask checks again and stops the message if it finds something you haven't protected.
  5. Showing you the real answer. When the AI replies, ThetaMask shows the real details in place of the stand-ins inside a sealed part of the page (a closed shadow root) that the AI website's own code cannot read. The website only ever holds stand-ins.

It does not ask for access to your browsing history, other websites, tabs, downloads, or your clipboard outside the AI sites.

What it measures

The popup shows how many items were hidden this week, by kind ("9 names, 6 emails"). Only the counts are stored, on your computer, never the values themselves.

How well it works

We test every change against synthetic prompts in English, Japanese and Bengali and a set of realistic law, accounting, recruiting, agency and support prompts. In the latest run:

  • 15 new real-world prompts, written after the last round of fixes: all 38 sensitive values caught.
  • Main real-world set: 118 of 119 values caught.
  • 1,200 synthetic prompts: no false alarms from the rules, and every real value restored in the answer.

What it can't do yet

  • Files and images you upload to the AI are not checked. Only text you paste or type is.
  • A company named on its own with no clue around it ("ask Dave at Northwind") can be missed. Add your clients to your always-hide list.
  • Japanese and Bengali names are found mainly by rules today; a larger optional model for both is coming.
  • It works in Chrome and Edge on ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini. Other browsers, other AI tools and phone apps are not covered yet.
  • It cannot stop someone deliberately turning it off or choosing "Send anyway".
